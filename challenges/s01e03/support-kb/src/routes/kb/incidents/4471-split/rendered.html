How to tell them apart

  • parser: the shared parser reads every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so day and month swap: a booking for 7 April lands on 4 July. Any channel.
  • hint: booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what it says, month first, and the form reads the date day first. Same swap, different bug: only the form channel of booking-v3 tenants, and fixing the parser does not fix it.
  • one-day: day and month stay put; the invite lands a day early or late, shows a different weekday to people in different places, or arrives before the booking was made. That is not date order. The desk noticed these (desk-notes.json) but filed them under parser by tenant.

Intake’s tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser. The tenant decides the cause, so the table counts by tenant, not by tag.

Lessons

  • A subject line tells you a report is about dates. It does not tell you which bug. Before you mark a big ticket known, split it by cause, not by subject.
  • A footnote under the wrong cause is still the wrong cause. If the desk reads a report differently from the rule, it is a problem of its own.
